## By night: the North Star

In the Northern Hemisphere, find the Big Dipper. The two stars at the outer edge of the "cup" are the **pointer stars**. Follow the line they make out of the cup about five times their spacing to a moderately bright star standing on its own. That's **Polaris**, and it sits within about a degree of true north.

Polaris isn't the brightest star in the sky. It's just the one that doesn't move.

## Close enough

- The sun rises roughly east and sets roughly west. "Roughly" can be off by 30° or more depending on season.
- At solar noon the sun is due south in most of the Northern Hemisphere (north in most of the Southern).
